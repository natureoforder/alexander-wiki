# Roadmap design

## Who it is for

Someone who has felt that some places are good to be in and others are not, and has heard Alexander's name, perhaps only through *A Pattern Language* or software patterns. The page opens on their question, not his vocabulary.

**The page has done its job if** a newcomer can follow six stops and then say what Alexander claimed, why it matters beyond architecture, and where to go next.

All stop names below are provisional until the concept list (`alex-88s.19.10`) is reviewed. Drawn from the digests for *The Nature of Order* 1–4 and *The Timeless Way* (all still in progress), Rufus's overview of *The Nature of Order* and his two talks. The *A Pattern Language* digest was not yet written.

Legend: ★ essential route, · other spine stop, "branch" optional depth. Text after a dash is the plain gloss the page would show.

## Candidate A: one question, followed down (recommended)

One spine in dependency order. Each act is the question the previous act raises. It follows the chain in Rufus's overview of *The Nature of Order*: places where we feel whole, so living things, so living processes, so why is that hard today. In practice it runs roughly through Books 1, 2, 3 and 4, with the pattern books brought in where they answer a question.

```
Why do some places make you feel more alive?
  [New: start at the top] [Came for patterns? go to the patterns stop] [Doubtful? where he is contested]

I   Is that feeling telling you something true?
    ★ the quality without a name — what good places share; later he calls it "life"
    · being alive — you know it first in yourself
    · the mirror of the self — a test you can run         branch: degrees of life
II  What is it in a place that you are feeling?
    ★ centers and wholeness — parts that strengthen each other
    ★ the fifteen properties — what living things have in common    branches: one each
    · living structure
III How does such a thing get made?
    ★ each step makes the whole more alive — the fifteen again, now as moves
      (unfolding wholeness)                               branch: structure-preserving transformations
    · one process, repeated — look, strengthen, look again          branches: every part unique, form language
    · patterns and pattern languages — shared knowledge of what works    branches: the gate, the 253 patterns
    · from language to sequences to codes — patterns put in order    branches: sequences, generative codes
IV  Who makes it, so that people belong there?
    ★ people shaping their own places                     branches: on-site decisions, Eishin, Mexicali
    · belonging — the world is ours and we may be in it
    · the rules that build the world — money, contracts, planning; changed one sequence at a time
V   Is the life in things also a kind of self?
    ★ the "I" — what he came to think lies underneath     branches: deep feeling, colour and inner light, ornament
After: why it matters beyond buildings — value as real; software; a Second Renaissance
```

- **For:** opens on experience; one chain of argument; "value is real" is met in act I, where Rufus's talks put it.
- **Against:** loses the drama of Alexander changing his mind. Patterns, which many visitors know him for, arrive at stop 9; the entrance link is the remedy. It is close to book order, so it asks the reader to take *The Nature of Order* as the frame.

## Candidate B: the story of a search

One spine in time. Each stop is an answer Alexander found and the problem it left open. Its hinge is that patterns fell short and *The Nature of Order* takes over from them.

```
One man, one question: how do you make things that are alive?

★ 1964–65  Can design be made rational?            Notes on the Synthesis of Form; "A City is Not a Tree"
★ 1975–79  Can ordinary people do it?              patterns, a pattern language, the quality without a name
· 1975–85  Does it work on the ground?             Oregon, Mexicali, Eishin; on-site decisions
★ 1975–2002 Why do good patterns still give dead places?    centers, the fifteen properties, form language
★ 2002     How is living structure made?           each step enhances the whole; sequences (~1990), generative codes (~2002)
· 2005     What would a whole world of it be like?   belonging; one process repeated; plans of public space after all
★ 2004     What is it all made of?                 the "I"
★ 2012     Why did the world resist?               the battle; contracts and money
```

- **For:** a story carries a reader further than a syllabus. It starts where many visitors already are (patterns, software) and gives them "the full story", the hook in Rufus's talk notes. It shows what is superseded, which A flattens.
- **Against:** a newcomer must care about the man before the ideas. We hold no text for several early works, so those stops lean on secondary sources. The tidy sequence is partly our construction and every date and turning point needs checking.

## Candidate C: three doors

No single spine. Three trails, chosen by purpose, each complete in itself.

```
What brings you here?

I want to make a place
  ★ people shaping their own places → patterns → centers → the fifteen properties → one step at a time → on-site decisions

I want the argument
  ★ the quality without a name → mirror of the self → living structure → each step enhances the whole → the "I"

I came from software
  ★ patterns → a pattern language → the gate → why patterns were not enough (centers) → generative codes
```

- **For:** short; nobody wades through what they did not come for; the software and builder audiences get a first-class entrance rather than a link.
- **Against:** concepts repeat across trails, and no single page order shows how making and meaning depend on each other.

## Check against the finished digests

All six digests are now in `natureoforder/sources/texts/<book>/digest.md`. They confirm the three candidates and A's order; changes made:

- **Patterns, then sequences, then codes.** Alexander's own staging, quoted in the *A Pattern Language* digest: pattern languages about 1967, sequences about 1990, generative codes about 2002. A now has a stop for it; B carries the dates.
- **Why patterns fell short**, the hinge of B, is in his words in Book 2: patterns become rules for generic centers (ch. 13), and patterns without a form language gave "funky" buildings (ch. 16 note 1). Form language added as a branch.
- **Book 2 Part Three** frames "why it is so hard now" as the rules of money, contracts and planning that generate the built world, changed "gene by gene" through small sequences. Act IV's last stop renamed to say so.
- **Book 3 revises Oregon**: piecemeal growth alone does not give large-scale order; a shared plan of public space is needed (p. 93). Noted in B; a branch candidate for A.
- **Book 1 replaces the quality without a name with "life" and wholeness.** A's first stop keeps the 1979 name as the door and says so in its gloss; the concept list should decide whether "being alive" stays a separate stop.

## Not proposed

The books in order. It is a reading list and belongs on the works hub (`alex-88s.19.4`).

## Recommendation

A, with C's doors reduced to entrance links at the top and B left for a later "Alexander's story" page that A links to.

**Essential six (A):** quality without a name; centers and wholeness; the fifteen properties; each step makes the whole more alive; people shaping their own places; the "I". Two say what it is, one how it is made, one who makes it, one what it means, after the felt opening. The open judgement is the "I": ending on it states his full claim, but some readers will balk.
