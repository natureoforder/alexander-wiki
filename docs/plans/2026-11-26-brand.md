# Alexander Wiki brand and look

This file starts as the brief. The feel, mood board, directions and Rufus's pick are added below as each is agreed. Method: the `designing-look-and-feel` skill. Session brief: [2026-11-26-design-session-brief.md](2026-11-26-design-session-brief.md).

## 1. Brief

### Goal

One look that feels like Alexander's own books, applied to the front page, pattern titles, navbar and global CSS. The reading pages already work (the *A Pattern Language* typesetting); the frame around them does not. The front page is plain, the pattern-title font is wrong, the navbar looks like a generic theme, and there is no rule for colour.

### Who it is for

- **Newcomers** arriving from *A Pattern Language*: they need to see quickly that this is a serious, beautiful place, and where to start.
- **Practitioners** coming back to look things up: they need fast navigation and pages that read and print well.
- **Readers who love the books**: they'll trust it only if it feels like the books.

### Kinds of page

| Page | What it needs from the look |
|---|---|
| Front page | Sets the feel. Pictures from the books, what the wiki is, and clear routes into concepts, patterns and practice. |
| Concept and practice pages | Long reading in the accepted body type: quotations, margin notes, figures. |
| Works index | Rufus: "design of works index page is important - could be by time but in a grid too and beautiful". 15 book covers (`assets/covers/`) and a few essays and talks, grouped into six periods, with a chronology table below. It needs a cover grid you take in at a glance that still reads as a timeline. |
| Index pages | Long lists to scan. Set them like the book's contents: numbers in oldstyle figures, names in small capitals, page references aligned right, hairline rules only. |
| Navbar and footer | Quiet and legible, in keeping with the books. |

### Fixed (not reopened)

- Body type after the *A Pattern Language* interior: EB Garamond, small-capital headings, oldstyle figures, generous spacing (`about/design.md`).
- Plate and commentary spread for patterns, stacked on phones.
- *Nature of Order* emblem as logo.
- Rejected: the red jacket palette, the earlier landing pages, the Seeds of Renaissance theme.

### Constraints

- **Flowershow.** Scoped CSS in `custom.css`, raw HTML in `layout: plain` pages, no scripts in `.md`. The navbar can only be restyled, so any design has to fit the markup Flowershow already outputs.
- **Merge-friendly.** Other sessions (works, concepts, Visionary) edit the same repo. Keep changes scoped and rebase on `main` before merging.
- **Light only, and print.** The site forces light mode, and that stays. Reading pages should print cleanly: no navbar, black text, figures kept.
- **Pictures from the books.** Use scans at modest size, each with a credit line (book, page, photographer where known), as the pattern pages already do. A large plate on the front page is the most visible use, so you should confirm you're comfortable with it.

### Open questions, with first answers

1. **Pattern-title font.** On p. 833 the title is "180 WINDOW PLACE\*\*": full capitals spaced about 0.1em apart, oldstyle figures, set flush left in line with the plate. Ours mixes Caslon with small capitals and is centred. The fault lies in that mix and the centring, not in the Garamond family. *First answer:* full EB Garamond capitals, widely spaced, flush left, with the number in oldstyle figures and the stars after it. As an alternative in the directions, test the face named in the 1977 edition's colophon, if a free version exists. Pattern cross-references in running text (ENTRANCE ROOM (130)) get the same treatment, in small capitals, as in the book.
2. **Navbar.** You said it "probably [needs] a different font". *First answer:* restyle it as a book's running head, with the emblem, the wiki name in spaced capitals, links in small capitals, one hairline rule beneath, and no grey band. Keep EB Garamond for now so the site has one family. The directions will also test a second face for the navbar alone.
3. **Front page.** Today it opens with a centred title, an italic line, a short text and a small Window Place picture. *First answer:* what changes is the **picture**, the **order** and the **routes**. One or more large plates from the books lead (chosen on the mood board), with the title set like the *A Pattern Language* title page beneath or beside them. The four routes become a short list set like a book's contents rather than cards.
4. **Works index.** Today it is a grid of cards per period. *First answer:* covers are shown at their true proportions on a shared baseline, like books on a shelf, with no card boxes. Each period is a row headed by its dates in oldstyle figures and its name in small capitals, so reading down the page is reading through time. Under each cover go the title and year in the book type. Essays and talks get a typeset title "cover" in the same proportion instead of a grey box. On phones, each period's row scrolls sideways or wraps two to a row. The mood board adds references for this: bookshop and publisher backlists, a catalogue raisonné. The directions show it as a fourth specimen. Classes stay `works-*`.
5. **Colour.** *First answer:* keep paper and ink quiet and let colour come from the pictures, as *The Nature of Order* does: plain pages, rich colour plates. Add at most one accent drawn from the books, either the warm yellow of the Oxford jacket or the deep blue of the *Nature of Order* covers. Give it one job only, for example the emblem and the rules, and check its contrast. The mood board tests whether this is enough.

### Feel (for your yes)

> **A well-made book you can walk into.**

- *Book:* the books' own typesetting, page rhythm and plates; plus the restraint of Tufte's pages, as you asked ("more tufte like or alexander like"). Nothing that looks like an app.
- *Well-made:* the care of the 1977 Oxford edition: spacing, proportion, capitals done properly.
- *Walk into:* it is about real places, so large, warm pictures of places and things lead.

**Not:** a SaaS site, an academic archive, a heritage brand with pastiche ornaments, a dark tech blog, Seeds of Renaissance.

### Method

1. This brief: you review it.
2. Mood board: 4–6 references and 1–2 anti-references, mostly the books themselves. That means the *A Pattern Language* interior and title page, the Oxford jacket, colour spreads and chapter openings from *Nature of Order* Book 4, and the *Nature of Order* covers, plus Tufte CSS. The anti-references are the rejected Seeds of Renaissance preview, and the current site is shown as "before". A subagent reviews the board before you react.
3. Directions: 2–3 specimens side by side, each showing a front-page opening, navbar, pattern title and palette, with one recommended. You pick.
4. Build the pick and check it in a browser at 1440 and 390 px wide.
