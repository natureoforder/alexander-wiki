# Alexander Wiki brand

The essence the whole site is judged against. The separate pieces of design work (navbar, pattern titles, Works index, front page) are in the [design brief](2026-11-26-design-brief.md).

## Essence

> **Handmade, and a pleasure to use.**

Alexander's books are not good because they are books. They are good because they were made with great care to be both beautiful and really good to use: well written, the text the right size, the spacing right, the pages laid out and headed with thought. *The Nature of Order* shows this most, with the effort that went into every spread. The wiki tries to do the same thing in its own medium, which is hypertext, not print.

- **Handmade.** You can feel care in the details: the fonts, spacing, capitals and captions. Alexander's photographs are often black and white and a little soft, and we show them as they are. Our own drawings use clean, fine lines in the manner of Co–Star; we do not imitate his pen.
- **Alexander's own pictures first.** Wherever a section has a relevant illustration in the books, including *The Nature of Order*, we reuse it with a credit. Reusing his pictures is one of the most valuable things the site can do.
- **A pleasure to use.** Useful first: fast to find things, easy to read on a phone and on a desktop. Being somewhat app-like is fine where that helps, and links, indexes and search should work the way the web does best. Like the books, its beauty comes from serving use.
- **Quiet, with colour where it earns its place.** Paper and ink are plain, and colour mostly comes from the pictures. A little colour from the books, such as the yellow of the *A Pattern Language* jacket, can appear at a few points. The directions try more than one way to do this.
- **Learn from the best, not by imitation.** We take the books' principles, not their page shapes. For type and spacing on screen, copy from sites that already do it really well.

**Character:** calm, warm, careful, plain, useful.

**Not:** a SaaS site, an academic archive, a pastiche of a printed book, heritage ornament, slick vector illustration, a Seeds of Renaissance look.

**Constraint:** Flowershow. Restyle what it gives us with scoped CSS, and don't overdo it.

## Voice

The house style is [docs/voice.md](../voice.md), and it governs every page, plan and agent. In short:

- **Introduce him.** Opening pages say who he was in one line: Christopher Alexander (1936–2022), the architect and thinker behind *A Pattern Language* and *The Nature of Order*.
- **Credit collaborators.** Most of the books and nearly all the buildings were made with others. "Alexander" is shorthand, and the front page and About page say so. Name co-authors where the work is theirs.
- **Close to his own voice.** Plain, concrete and unhurried, about real places and feelings. No hype, jargon or marketing. Our words are short and serve his.

## What is already settled

- Reading type follows the inside of *A Pattern Language*: EB Garamond, small-capital headings, oldstyle figures, generous spacing (`about/design.md`).
- Patterns open with the plate beside the commentary, stacked on phones.
- The *Nature of Order* emblem is the logo.
- Rejected: the red jacket palette, the first landing pages, the Seeds of Renaissance theme.

## Decision (Rufus, 26 Nov)

From the [directions page](https://claude.ai/artifact/4S7VbuKXFE31Fib1g4oKAE) (references in [brand/](brand/)):

- **Direction B, Field notes.** EB Garamond for everything you read, with small capitals for titles and cross-references. A small monospaced face (DM Mono, uppercase, 11–12 px) is used only for the apparatus: navbar links, pattern numbers, page references, years.
- **Paper stays white.** No black bands ("not sure about the black background"). Where a page needs a colour field, use the jacket yellow, as in C's Works index ("the coloring in option C for the book background looked kind of nice").
- **Colour:** quiet paper, with colour coming from the pictures.
- **No hand-drawn rules** ("just looks bad… kind of messy").
- **Our own drawings follow Co–Star, not Alexander's hand.** Fine, clean line drawings in Co–Star's manner. We don't imitate his pen. His own diagrams are still reused wherever they fit.
- **The logo stays.**
- **Works index:** B's period rows, plus C's sideways shelf of all the books ("very cool"), with the chronology below.

## Later decisions (Rufus, 26 Nov)

- **Reading text size:** 19.2 px at desktop width, scaling down to about 17 px on phones (`clamp(1.06rem, 1.33vw, 1.2rem)`). It matches the front-page opening. 16.8 px was too small, and 20.8 px was the size before Direction B.
- **Front page plates:** three black and white plates under "Where to begin", each linking to its subject: Levels of scale (the Isfahan porch), Laying out Eishin (the flags), and Centers (the Anatolian carpet). Each shows its own colour on hover. The rest of the front page stays as it was. Keep the number of choices low (see the rejected [front page mockups](brand/front-page-mockups/README.md)).
- **Contents rail:** hairline ticks, with the first one level with the page title. The outline is set like Alexander's contents pages.
- **Comparisons:** each one goes on a page of its own, not on the look-and-feel page.
