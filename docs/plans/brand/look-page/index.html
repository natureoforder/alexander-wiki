Alexander Wiki: look and feel

The books as the reference, one web reference, and three directions to choose from.

Handmade, and a pleasure to use.

Alexander’s books are good because they were made with great care to be both beautiful and really good to use. The wiki tries to do the same in hypertext. It should show care in the details and draw with the hand. It uses Alexander’s own pictures first, it is useful first, and it stays quiet, with colour where it earns its place.

Decisions for you

1. Which direction, or which blend?

Recommended: B, Field notes, with C’s hand-drawn rules. B is the most clearly web-native and follows your one reference (Co–Star). C’s rules add the hand. If the mono feels too cold, take A with C’s rules.

2. Colour

Recommended: quiet paper, with colour coming from the pictures. Use the jacket yellow in at most one or two places, such as the Works index, and never in the navbar. Leave out the blue.

3. Works index layout

Recommended: B’s period rows, with C’s single shelf as a compact strip at the top of the page so you can see all fifty years at a glance. The chronology table stays below.

4. Pattern titles

All three set the title in spaced full EB Garamond capitals, flush left, as on p. 833. This replaces today’s mix of Caslon and small capitals. B adds the number and page as a small mono line above. Recommended: B’s.
