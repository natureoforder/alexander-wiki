# How we speak about Alexander

House style for every page, plan and agent working on the wiki.

## Alexander and his collaborators

Most of Alexander's books and nearly all his buildings were made with others. *A Pattern Language* has six authors. *The Oregon Experiment*, *The Production of Houses*, *A New Theory of Urban Design* and *Building Living Neighborhoods* were all collaborations, and the Center for Environmental Structure was a working group, not one man's office.

- **"Alexander" is shorthand, and we say so.** The wiki is the Alexander Wiki and it is natural to write "Alexander argues…". The About page and the front page state plainly that the work is his and his collaborators', so the shorthand need not be repeated everywhere.
- **Name collaborators where the work is theirs.** On works pages, list every author. When quoting a co-authored book, cite it by its authors (e.g. "Alexander, Ishikawa and Silverstein, *A Pattern Language*") on first mention on a page; after that, the title alone is enough. On project pages (Eishin, Mexicali, Living Neighborhoods), name the people who did the work.
- **"In his own words".** Use it, since it reads well and *The Nature of Order* and *The Timeless Way* are single-author. Where a page quotes co-authored books, write "in their own words" or "in the authors' own words".

## Introducing him

Do not assume the reader knows who he was. Opening pages (home, About, Visionary, roadmap) say it in one line: Christopher Alexander (1936–2022), the architect and thinker behind *A Pattern Language* and *The Nature of Order*.

## Tone

- Close to his own voice: plain, concrete, unhurried, about real places and feelings. No hype, no academic jargon, no marketing language.
- Our words are short and serve his. Label our own examples as ours.
- Be honest about where he is contested or unsure, as he was himself.
