<h1 class="ar-title">A roadmap through Christopher Alexander</h1>
<p class="ar-lede">Christopher Alexander (1936–2022) was the architect and thinker behind <em>A Pattern Language</em> and <em>The Nature of Order</em>. Why do some places make you feel more alive? He spent fifty years on that question. This is a way into his answer, one question at a time, each opening the next.</p>

<div class="ar-act" id="act-i"><div class="ar-act__num ar-sc">Act I</div><h2 class="ar-act__title">Is that feeling telling you something true?</h2><p class="ar-act__sub">Everyone knows that some places feel more alive than others. Alexander’s first and largest claim is that this feeling is not just taste: it registers something real, in the place and in us.</p><hr class="ar-act__rule" /></div>

What the best places share: nothing in them is at war with itself. In 1979 he said it could not be named; by 2002 he called it simply life, and said that everything has more of it or less.</p></div>
