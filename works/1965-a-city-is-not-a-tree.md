# A City is Not a Tree

Essay · 1965 · *Architectural Forum* vol. 122 no. 1 (April 1965) and no. 2 (May 1965); reprinted in *Design* no. 206 (February 1966)

## What it is

A two-part essay contrasting "natural" cities, which grew over time, with "artificial" cities and neighbourhoods laid out by planners. Alexander argues that the planned city is organised as a *tree*, in the mathematical sense: every unit belongs to exactly one larger unit, so that nothing overlaps. Real urban life is organised as a *semilattice*, in which units overlap and connect across categories, as when a newsstand, a traffic light and a pavement together make a small place where people wait and meet. Imposing a tree on a city, he argues, cuts it apart.

## Why it matters

Alexander's most widely cited essay, still read in planning, network science and software. It marks his turn from the decomposition method of *Notes on the Synthesis of Form*, which produced tree-like hierarchies, towards overlapping structure, the line of thought that leads to the network of patterns and later to the overlapping centers of *The Nature of Order*.

## Source and credits

The essay is © the Alexander estate and the original publishers, and the wiki does not reproduce it. Two copies are freely readable:

- **The original printing:** scans of the full April and May 1965 issues of *Architectural Forum* are held by [USModernist](https://usmodernist.org/forum.htm). Part I is on printed pages 58–62 of the [April 1965 issue](https://usmodernist.org/AF/AF-1965-04.pdf) (pages 56–60 of the PDF); Part II is on printed pages 58–61 of the [May 1965 issue](https://usmodernist.org/AF/AF-1965-05.pdf) (pages 58–61 of the PDF). The usual citation gives 58–62 for Part II, but the essay ends on page 61 and page 62 begins another article. The scans carry all of the original figures: the tree and semilattice diagrams, the overlapping-set drawings, and the plans of Columbia, Greenbelt, Chandigarh, Brasilia, the Tokyo plan and the other planned cities Alexander discusses.
- **The Center for Environmental Structure text:** the full essay, with most of the figures, at [PatternLanguage.com](https://www.patternlanguage.com/archive/cityisnotatree.html), the site of the organisation Alexander founded.
