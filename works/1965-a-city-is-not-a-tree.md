# A City is Not a Tree

Essay · 1965 · *Architectural Forum* vol. 122 no. 1 (April 1965) and no. 2 (May 1965); reprinted in *Design* no. 206 (February 1966)

## What it is

A two-part essay contrasting "natural" cities, which grew over time, with "artificial" cities and neighbourhoods laid out by planners. Alexander argues that the planned city is organised as a *tree*, in the mathematical sense: every unit belongs to exactly one larger unit, so that nothing overlaps. Real urban life is organised as a *semilattice*, in which units overlap and connect across categories, as when a newsstand, a traffic light and a pavement together make a small place where people wait and meet. Imposing a tree on a city, he argues, cuts it apart.

## Why it matters

Alexander's most widely cited essay, still read in planning, network science and software. It marks his turn from the decomposition method of *Notes on the Synthesis of Form*, which produced tree-like hierarchies, towards overlapping structure, the line of thought that leads to the network of patterns and later to the overlapping centers of *The Nature of Order*.
