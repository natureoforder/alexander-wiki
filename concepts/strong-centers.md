# Strong centers

What gives a gathering place its focus? A tree can play a part, but so can the benches facing it, the path that ends there, and the patch of shade they all share. Each can make the others more distinct. Alexander uses strong centers to describe this reciprocal effect.[^1]

Alexander examines an Anatolian carpet in which the surrounding figures help to establish the force of the center. Try a similar observation in a room: what would you have to move at its edges before its middle lost its character?

See also [centers](centers.md) and [the fifteen properties](fifteen-properties.md).

[^1]: Christopher Alexander, *The Nature of Order*, Book One, *The Phenomenon of Life*, chapter 5, pp. 151–157.
