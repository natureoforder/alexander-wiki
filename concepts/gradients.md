# Gradients

A succession of changes can disclose an underlying direction or pressure. On a hillside, buildings might step down as the ground falls; near a public entrance a path might broaden as more people join it. In either case the changing dimensions tell you something about the place.[^1]

Walk from a busy street toward the quietest room in a house. Where do the sound, light, or scale of the openings change by degrees? Where does one change more abruptly than the others?

See also [contrast](contrast.md) and [the fifteen properties](fifteen-properties.md).

[^1]: Christopher Alexander, *The Nature of Order*, Book One, *The Phenomenon of Life*, chapter 5, pp. 205–209.
