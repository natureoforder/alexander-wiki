# Contrast

A bright opening is easier to see from a shaded passage. The passage, in turn, takes its character from the light at its end. For Alexander, contrast matters when the difference between two centers helps both register more strongly.[^1]

Look from a doorway into a garden. Cover the dark jamb with your hand. Does that change the shape or brilliance of the patch of garden you see? The contrast belongs to the relationship between them.

[^1]: Christopher Alexander, *The Nature of Order*, Book One, *The Phenomenon of Life*, chapter 5, pp. 200–204.
