# The fifteen properties

Look at a doorway with a deep reveal. Its sides give the opening a clear shape. The opening gives the wall a focus. A step spanning the reveal makes a small place where someone can sit. In a few square feet, several parts of a building have become more distinct and more useful through their relation to one another.

Alexander spent years comparing objects and places he thought had this kind of life. Fifteen recurrent features emerged. He came to understand them as ways that [centers](centers.md) can strengthen one another. A boundary works because its two sides gain something from it. Repetition works when the intervals take on life too. He presents the properties separately so the reader can learn to see them, then returns to how much they depend on each other.[^1]

## Look at the same place twice

Try the doorway again with just two properties in mind. First draw its **boundary**, including the depth of the step. Then draw the **positive space** on either side of it. In the second drawing a detail you took for decoration may turn out to shape the place a person occupies while waiting for the door to open. This is one way to use Alexander’s vocabulary to discover something you had overlooked.
