# Roughness

Alexander’s example of a decorated Persian bowl poses a practical question: what happens where a repeating design must fit a surface that does not divide evenly? Small adjustments let the maker keep the pattern coherent around the bowl. The irregularities record what the particular place required.[^1]

Imagine laying a brick border around an old tree. A slight change of angle at each joint might hold the curve more faithfully than a row of identical joints with one awkward gap at the end.

See also [alternating repetition](alternating-repetition.md) and [the fifteen properties](fifteen-properties.md).

[^1]: Christopher Alexander, *The Nature of Order*, Book One, *The Phenomenon of Life*, chapter 5, pp. 210–217.
