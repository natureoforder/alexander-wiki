# Simplicity and inner calm

An arrangement can contain many parts and still be quiet to look at. In Alexander’s account, that calm emerges as each part comes to serve the whole and the maker can let unnecessary complications go. Counting how few elements remain gives little indication of whether the whole has settled.[^1]

Try the question on a shelf of everyday objects. Move one item at a time. Which move allows the other objects to stand more easily together, and which merely leaves a bigger gap?

[^1]: Christopher Alexander, *The Nature of Order*, Book One, *The Phenomenon of Life*, chapter 5, pp. 226–229.
