# Deep feeling

During the planning of the Eishin school in Japan, a teacher found it difficult to say what kind of campus he wanted. Pressed to imagine a moment in his own day, he described going to a place with water between classes. Other members of the school recognized something of their own wishes in his answer. A waterside place entered the written pattern language while its shape and position were still open.[^1]

Alexander calls the capacity to apprehend a situation in this way **deep feeling**. You can sometimes recognize the quality a place needs before you can draw the place. The feeling gives the next design decision a test. If you tried putting the water beside the busiest entrance, would a teacher coming out of class still find the few minutes of quiet that prompted the idea? The entrance example is a way to try Alexander’s question on the school’s brief.[^2]

## When two wishes pull apart

The teachers wanted the high-school classrooms to feel like separate homes. They also wanted to reach the next class without getting wet. In Alexander’s account the disagreement took some arguing. A separate building with a garden meant going outdoors. He reports asking the teachers again after they had experienced the campus for a year. They had come to enjoy going out between classes, even in the rain. The return visit matters. It gave the people who used the school a chance to judge what the decision had actually made of their day.[^3]
