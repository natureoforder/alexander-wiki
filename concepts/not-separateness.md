# Not-separateness

A garden gate can be unmistakably itself and still seem to belong to its lane. Its scale, the way the hedge meets its posts, and the approach worn into the ground all carry relationships across its edge. Alexander uses not-separateness for this felt continuity between a thing and its world.[^1]

Look for a place where removing a modest element would change something on both sides of it. The gate may matter as much to the character of the public lane as it does to the garden behind it.

See also [echoes](echoes.md) and [the fifteen properties](fifteen-properties.md).

[^1]: Christopher Alexander, *The Nature of Order*, Book One, *The Phenomenon of Life*, chapter 5, pp. 230–235.
