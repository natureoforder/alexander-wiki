# Boundaries

A boundary can have enough depth to become a place in its own right. A porch, for instance, gives a house an edge where someone can sit in shelter and still talk to a neighbor outside. Both the room behind it and the street in front acquire a more definite shape.[^1]

In Alexander’s illustrations, follow the surround of a doorway. Notice how its smaller parts give the opening weight, and how the opening gives those parts something to enclose.

See also [deep interlock and ambiguity](deep-interlock-and-ambiguity.md) and [the fifteen properties](fifteen-properties.md).

[^1]: Christopher Alexander, *The Nature of Order*, Book One, *The Phenomenon of Life*, chapter 5, pp. 158–164.
