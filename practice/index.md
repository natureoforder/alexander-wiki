# Practice

What can you learn about a place by walking it that you would miss on a drawing? Begin with a visit to a place you know, or follow one design decision from a school’s brief to the Japanese hillside where the school was built.

## Lay out a place on the ground

The Eishin school needed a street for its high-school classrooms and a center for its college. For months the team could see how to join them on paper and still had no answer that fit the land. Read how a ridge changed the plan, then try two arrangements for a gathering place at full size. [Laying out the Eishin campus](eishin-campus.md)

## Make a neighborhood diagnosis

Take a neighbor and a map. Walk separately long enough to notice different things, then compare the places each of you would protect or change. The exercise leads into the original Living Neighborhoods workbooks. [A first walk](../living-neighborhoods/first-walk.md)

## Choose what comes first

If you place a building before its garden, the garden inherits the available space. Alexander’s proposal for Pasadena starts by choosing the garden’s position. See how changing the order of decisions affects what a group can build. [Generative codes](../concepts/generative-codes.md)
